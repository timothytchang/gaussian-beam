Model and conventions

The envelope shows the beam radius in two independent transverse axes. For Gaussian profiles this is the 1/e² intensity radius. Horizontal and vertical plot scales differ.

Propagation uses the paraxial ABCD law, q′ = (Aq + B)/(Cq + D), with 1/q = 1/R − i M²λ/(πnw²). Wavelength is the vacuum wavelength. All geometry uses millimeters.

Positive focal length is converging. A mirror’s positive radius is focusing; reflection unfolds into the forward bench axis. For a refractive interface, positive radius has its center of curvature downstream. Curved mirrors use normal incidence.

Drag an optic in the plot to move it, or edit its position below. Click the plot to move the probe. Arrow keys on a focused optic move it by 1 mm; hold Shift for 10 mm.

Local waist values extrapolate within the probe’s current medium, ignoring subsequent optics. They can lie outside that segment. The cross section shows peak-normalized intensity; it does not estimate transmitted power.

The model excludes apertures, clipping, aberrations, nonparaxial focusing, diffraction from edges, polarization, and oblique astigmatism. For M² > 1 it propagates an effective second-moment envelope; the displayed Gaussian intensity is illustrative.
